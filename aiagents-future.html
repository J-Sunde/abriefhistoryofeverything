1950
1950 — 1980

The Talking Machine

Before neural networks or language models, researchers built systems that could hold conversations through rules, scripts, and symbolic logic. The seeds of the agent idea were planted here.

Turing's Imitation Game

Alan Turing proposes a test for machine intelligence: can a machine converse so convincingly that an interrogator cannot distinguish it from a human? The question frames AI research for decades.

ELIZA — The First Chatbot

Joseph Weizenbaum at MIT creates ELIZA using pattern matching. Users form emotional connections to a machine running 200 lines of code. Weizenbaum is disturbed by what he's made.

PARRY — A Paranoid Mind

Kenneth Colby creates PARRY, simulating paranoid schizophrenia. In blind trials, psychiatrists cannot reliably distinguish PARRY from real patients.

The First AI Winter

Funding dries up as early AI promises outpace results. The Lighthill Report and DARPA's frustrations trigger severe cuts. The gap between ambition and capability is exposed.

1986
1986 — 2012

The Neural Turn

Backpropagation revives neural networks. LSTMs learn sequences. The field crawls, then runs — culminating in the ImageNet breakthrough that resets all expectations.

Backpropagation

Rumelhart, Hinton, and Williams popularise backpropagation. Multi-layer neural networks can learn from errors. The theoretical foundation for modern deep learning is laid.

LSTM

Hochreiter and Schmidhuber introduce Long Short-Term Memory networks. LSTMs dominate speech and text for the next twenty years.

ImageNet

Fei-Fei Li publishes ImageNet — 14 million labelled images. It becomes the benchmark that triggers a revolution when AlexNet attacks it at scale.

AlexNet Moment

AlexNet halves the ImageNet error rate. Deep learning — not hand-crafted features — will define the next decade of AI.

“
A computer would deserve to be called intelligent if it could deceive a human into believing that it was human.— Alan Turing · 1950
2017
2017 — 2020

Attention Is All You Need

A single paper abandons recurrence entirely. The transformer propagates through language, vision, audio, and code — becoming the foundation of every AI agent that follows.

The Transformer Paper

Vaswani et al. publish "Attention Is All You Need." Within two years it dominates every NLP benchmark. One paper. Ten thousand products built on top of it.

BERT & GPT-1

Google's BERT sets records on eleven NLP tasks. OpenAI's GPT-1 demonstrates that pre-training on web text transfers to downstream tasks.

GPT-2 — Too Dangerous?

OpenAI stages GPT-2's release citing misuse concerns. The first mainstream debate about AI safety and responsible disclosure.

GPT-3 — Scale Changes Everything

175 billion parameters. Few-shot learning, code generation, analogy reasoning. The era of large language models as platforms begins.

2022
2021 — 2022

The Consumer Breakthrough

RLHF transforms powerful but unpredictable base models into aligned, conversational agents. ChatGPT creates the fastest product adoption in consumer technology history.

DALL·E

Zero-shot image synthesis from text. Generative AI crosses into the visual domain and the public gets their first real taste of what's coming.

InstructGPT & RLHF

Human feedback on a fraction of GPT-3's parameters produces a dramatically more helpful model. RLHF becomes the standard alignment technique.

ChatGPT Launches

One million users in five days. One hundred million in two months. The AI agent enters the mainstream. Permanently.

Stable Diffusion Goes Open

Stability AI releases weights publicly. AI image generation is decentralised. The copyright debate starts and doesn't stop.

“
We are at a ChatGPT moment for AI. The question is no longer whether these systems work — it is what we do with them.— Sam Altman · 2023
2023
2023 — 2024

The Multimodal Race

Every major technology company deploys its frontier model. The Big 5 crystallise. Five distinct positions on safety, creativity, openness, and the speed of release.

GPT-4 & Claude 1

OpenAI releases GPT-4 with vision. Anthropic launches Claude 1 — Constitutional AI, thoughtful responses over speed. Two philosophies made public in the same week.

Grok Enters

xAI launches Grok with real-time X data. The first frontier AI built to have an editorial point of view.

Gemini

Google DeepMind releases Gemini, natively multimodal. A misleading demo triggers a debate about AI marketing versus reality.

Open Source Eruption

Meta releases Llama 2 and Llama 3. Mistral and Phi approach frontier performance. The open vs. closed debate intensifies everywhere.

2025
2024 — 2025

The Agentic Turn

Language models gain tools, memory, and the ability to act. The shift from assistant to agent reframes the entire field.

Tool Use & Function Calling

Structured tool-use APIs arrive across all major labs. The model becomes an orchestrator, not just a responder.

Claude 3 — Haiku / Sonnet / Opus

Anthropic demonstrates model families that segment by speed and intelligence. Claude 3 Opus briefly tops benchmarks for reasoning.

Gemini 1.5 — 1M Tokens

One million token context window. An entire codebase in a single prompt. Long-context reasoning becomes the next competitive front.

Computer Use & Autonomous Agents

Claude gains computer use. OpenAI releases Operator. AI agents complete multi-hour tasks without human checkpoints. No longer theoretical.

Ten architects

The people who built
the agent era

1950s
Alan Turing
Mathematician · Theorist

Proposed the imitation game. His 1950 paper framed the philosophical foundation of AI research for the entire field.

1960s
Joseph Weizenbaum
MIT · Creator of ELIZA

Built the first chatbot in 1966. Disturbed by users' emotional attachment to ELIZA. Became an early critic of uncritical AI enthusiasm.

1980s–2020s
Geoffrey Hinton
Deep Learning Pioneer

Co-developed backpropagation. AlexNet triggered the current era. Left Google in 2023 to speak freely about existential AI risk. Nobel Laureate 2024.

2017
Ashish Vaswani
Google Brain · Transformer

Lead author of "Attention Is All You Need." His work underpins every major AI agent. One paper. Ten thousand products.

2019–present
Sam Altman
CEO · OpenAI

Guided OpenAI from research lab to the world's most influential AI company. His brief firing and reinstatement in 2023 became Silicon Valley's most dramatic moment.

2021–present
Dario Amodei
CEO · Anthropic

Left OpenAI to build AI differently. Developed Constitutional AI — the training methodology behind Claude. A leading voice on existential AI risk.

2010–present
Demis Hassabis
CEO · Google DeepMind

Founded DeepMind, led AlphaGo's defeat of Lee Sedol, oversaw the merger creating Google DeepMind. Nobel Laureate in Chemistry 2024 for AlphaFold.

2022–present
David Holz
Founder · Midjourney

Built Midjourney as a tiny, profitable, bootstrapped company while competitors raised billions. Community-driven, Discord-native. The anti-VC AI company.

2015–present
Elon Musk
Co-founder OpenAI · xAI

Co-founded OpenAI, departed, sued it. Founded xAI and launched Grok. Acquisition of X gave xAI a data and distribution advantage no competitor can replicate.

2009–present
Fei-Fei Li
Stanford · ImageNet

Created ImageNet. Catalysed the deep learning revolution. The dataset that started everything — built by hand, at enormous scale, when no one else was looking.

What comes next

Four trajectories
emerging now

Persistent Autonomous Agents

AI agents that run for days or weeks — managing projects, hiring contractors, executing strategies. The question shifts from "can it do this?" to "can I trust it unsupervised?"

Consolidation of the Big 5

Midjourney into video. Grok deeper into X. ChatGPT and Gemini fight for enterprise. Claude pursues healthcare, law, and government. The map hardens.

Memory & Identity

Persistent memory and richer user profiles collapse the distinction between assistant and personalised agent. Regulation lags product reality by years.

Safety vs. Speed

The central tension is not technical — it is governance. Who decides when an autonomous agent has too much power? Written in real-time across labs, legislatures, and boardrooms.
